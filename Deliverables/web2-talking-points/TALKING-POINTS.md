# Gold's Gym CEO Presentation — Talking Points Breakdown

**Date:** March 12, 2026
**Presenters:** Mike (relationship/sales lead) + Matt (technical depth/architecture)

## Context

Mike leads as the relationship/sales person. Matt provides technical depth, industry experience, and drives home two key strategic messages: (1) Gold's must own their member experience, and (2) Gold's must own their data layer for AI/automation readiness.

**Strategic framing note:** The presentation recommends the Assemble model as the right starting point given Gold's current position. Build should be presented respectfully — it's a strong model, just one that requires more organizational readiness. Don't dismiss build; position assemble as the natural first step that doesn't preclude building key components over time as the organization matures. The architecture (owned data layer, owned touchpoints) is the same regardless — the question is just where vendors fit in vs. custom.

## Page 5: Build vs. Assemble vs. Buy (build-assemble-buy.html)

### MIKE — Frames the decision simply

- "There are really only three paths forward. Let me walk you through them."
- **BUILD**: Custom platform, full control, highest investment. "This gives you the most control and differentiation. It's a powerful model — but it requires significant organizational readiness, a dedicated engineering team, and a longer runway."
- **BUY**: Single vendor, fastest but limiting. "No single vendor covers everything Gold's needs. You'd be buying gaps."
- **ASSEMBLE** (recommended starting point): Best-in-class vendors integrated through a layer you own. "This matches where Gold's is today — asset-light, franchise-growth focused. And importantly, it doesn't lock you out of building custom components down the road where it makes strategic sense."
- **Important framing**: Don't position build as wrong — position it as aspirational. Assemble is the pragmatic starting point that builds toward the same architecture. "The good news is, the architecture is the same either way — owned data layer, owned member touchpoints. The question is how you get there."

### MATT — Adds the org and revenue angle

- **Org requirements**: Build needs 8/10 team investment, Assemble needs 5/10, Buy needs 2/10. "Assemble lets you start lean and grow capability over time. As your team matures and you learn which components give you the most competitive advantage, you can selectively build where it makes the biggest impact."
- **Revenue opportunity** — this is a big moment: "Gold's currently collects **$0 in technology fees**. Your competitors charge $5+ per join, monthly tech fees, vendor rebates. This is a revenue center you're leaving on the table."
- Walk the services table: Join Online, Mobile App, CRM, BI, Travel Pass — current state vs. future state vs. revenue model
- "Whether you assemble or build, this revenue opportunity is there. The architecture unlocks it either way."

## Page 6: Our Recommendation (recommendation.html)

### MIKE — Paints the vision

- Start with the **"Gold's Strong Model"** framework:
  - What Gold's IS: Strength/performance brand, franchise-growth company, premium pricing ($49.95-$69.95+), lean tech-enabled operations
  - What Gold's IS NOT: Casual fitness, HVLP competitor, big-box amenities
- "Everything we're recommending is in service of this identity."
- Walk the **5 Strategic Pillars**: Strength Dominance, Recovery as Revenue, Lean Operations (GM + AGM model), Unified Platform, Premium Positioning
- "This isn't a technology project. It's a business transformation that happens to require technology."

### MATT — Delivers the technical architecture ★★

- **This is Matt's biggest moment in the presentation.**
- Walk the **component table**:
  - Core Gym Management → Competitive RFP (evaluate ABC and alternatives)
  - Group Exercise → Motion Vibe is currently the highest-rated system at 4.5/5; "we'll evaluate this more thoroughly in Phase 1 to confirm it's the right long-term fit, but early signals are positive"
  - CRM & Marketing → Upgrade to fitness-specific with SMS
  - CDP → Add unified member data platform (**NEW — this is the data ownership play**)
  - Member App → Consolidate 20-30+ fragmented apps into ONE branded experience
  - Access Control → Standardize across all locations
  - Data & Analytics → Build data lake + BI dashboards

- **OWN YOUR DATA LAYER** — "This is the single most strategic decision you'll make. The data layer is where AI lives. It's where automation lives. It's where personalization lives. If you let vendors own this, you're renting your future."
  - Walk the 3-layer architecture diagram:
    - Top: Member-facing touchpoints (app, portal, dashboards, website) — **you own these**
    - Middle: Gold's Data & Intelligence Layer (unified data model, analytics, AI, open API) — **you own this**
    - Bottom: Vendor systems of record (they do the heavy lifting, you control the integration)
  - "The middle layer is the moat. It's what lets you switch vendors without losing your data. It's what lets you deploy AI agents that work across all your systems. It's what turns Gold's from a franchise that uses technology into a technology-enabled franchise platform."
  - **Plant the seed**: "And over time, as you build capability in this layer, you may find that building certain components yourself — rather than integrating vendors — gives you an even bigger competitive edge. The architecture supports that evolution naturally."

- **OWN YOUR MEMBER TOUCHPOINTS** — "When a member opens the Gold's app, that should be YOUR experience, not ABC's, not a white-label. Every interaction a member has with your brand should feel like Gold's."

- **Technology Decision Council** — recommend a governance body so these decisions don't get fragmented again

### MATT — Addresses risks and RFP rigor

- Walk the **RFP Evaluation Scorecard** — weighted criteria:
  - Member Experience (25%), Core Gym Management (20%), Franchise Ops (15%), CRM (15%), Data & Analytics (10%), Fitness/PT (8%), Access (5%), Back Office (2%)
- "We're not picking vendors based on demos and lunches. This is a structured, scored evaluation. And for each component, we'll also evaluate whether a custom-build approach could deliver better results — it's not just about which vendor, it's about which approach."
- **Risk Factors** — be transparent:
  - Franchisee Resistance → Corporate pilot + FOMO, not mandates
  - Timeline Pressure → Quick wins (app, website) build trust early

  - International Complexity → Domestic first, international Phase 7+
  - Resource Constraints → Assemble model is designed to be lean initially, with room to grow

## General Presentation Tips

- **Mike's role**: Storytelling, relationship, business case, vision, next steps/close
- **Matt's role**: Data, architecture, competitive intelligence, technical credibility, AI/future urgency

### Key Matt Moments (star these for emphasis)

1. **Page 4**: Competitive insight — everyone assembles, but the best ones evolve toward building key components
2. **Page 6**: Architecture diagram — own the data layer, own the touchpoints (same architecture regardless of build vs. assemble)
3. **Page 6**: AI/automation urgency — data ownership = future-proofing
4. **Page 6**: Plant the seed — "as you grow capability, building key components gives even more edge"
5. **Page 7**: Risk transparency — builds trust

### Pacing

- **Don't linger** on Pages 2-3 (Discovery/Survey) — the CEOs will want to get to the "so what" quickly. Use these as credibility builders, not deep dives.
- **Spend the most time** on Pages 5-6 (Build/Assemble/Buy and Recommendation) — this is where decisions happen.
- **Page 7** should feel like confidence, not overwhelm — "we've thought through the risks and the path is clear."

### Strategic Threading (Subtle)

Throughout the presentation, plant these seeds without hammering them:

1. **"The architecture is the same either way"** — whether you assemble or build, you need the same owned data layer and owned touchpoints. This makes the build conversation in Phase 1 & 2 feel like a natural evolution, not a pivot.
2. **"Start here, evolve from here"** — assemble isn't the destination, it's the on-ramp. As Gold's builds organizational capability, the conversation about building custom components will happen naturally.
3. **"Evaluate each component on its merits"** — don't lock into "we assemble everything." Phase 1 & 2 will assess each component individually. Some may warrant a build approach.
4. **Motion Vibe** — cite the high score but frame as "early signal, needs deeper evaluation." Don't commit to keeping it. Phase 1 will do the thorough assessment.
