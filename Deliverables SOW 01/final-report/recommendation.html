01 VISION OF GOLD'S GYM

The Gold's Strong Model defines what this brand is — and what it is not. Every technology decision must pass through this framework.

WHAT GOLD'S IS
  • A strength and performance brand with 50+ years of heritage
  • A franchise-growth company targeting 16-25 new locations annually
  • A premium experience priced above HVLP competitors ($55-70+)
  • A lean, technology-enabled operation (GM + AGM staffing model)
WHAT GOLD'S IS NOT
  • A general-purpose fitness center for casual exercisers
  • A low-price, high-volume (HVLP) competitor
  • A big-box gym with pools, spas, and childcare
  • A corporate-gym-focused business
FIVE STRATEGIC PILLARS
1. Strength Dominance

Massive free weight zones, Olympic platforms, performance turf, HYROX-style training. No pools. No childcare. No HVLP dilution.

2. Recovery as Revenue

Cold plunge, infrared sauna, red light therapy, compression systems. Tier-gated to drive premium membership adoption.

3. Lean Operations

GM + AGM only. Technology must reduce headcount, not create it. Self-service check-in, booking, and payment are mandatory.

4. Unified Platform

One membership system, one app, one data model. Brand controls with franchise flexibility. Real-time royalty visibility.

5. Premium Positioning

2x HVLP pricing ($49.95-$69.95+). Attracts tier-one franchise candidates who expect turnkey operations and strong unit economics.

Facility Template

~30,000 sq ft · QR/gate access · Recovery suite · Performance studio · Content zones

TECHNOLOGY GOVERNANCE

Technology must be a central voice in product and business decisions — not an afterthought. Gold's should establish a Technology Decision Council: a cross-functional body including Technology, Product, Marketing, and Operations leadership that guides platform decisions, vendor selection, and roadmap prioritization.

Open question: Where do MarTech and Product sit organizationally? Under Technology or Marketing? This is a Phase 1 decision that shapes the governance model.

02 THE ASSEMBLE MODEL

Assemble best-in-class components connected by an integration layer that Gold's owns. Each component is selected for its category strength, not forced into a monolithic suite.

OWN YOUR DATA LAYER

Interfaces come and go. Vendors come and go. The data and integration layer connecting these components is the most strategic asset Gold's will own. It controls how member data flows between systems, prevents vendor lock-in, and ensures any individual component can be upgraded or replaced without disrupting the whole. In an era where AI, automation, and analytics all depend on clean, unified, accessible data — owning this layer is what creates optionality.

WHY THE DATA LAYER MATTERS
Vendor Portability

Any vendor can be swapped without rebuilding. Your data model and API contracts survive vendor transitions intact.

AI & Automation Readiness

AI agents, predictive analytics, and automated workflows all require clean, unified, accessible data. The integration layer is the prerequisite.

Franchise Scalability

One data model across 400+ locations means consistent reporting, real-time royalty visibility, and standardized member experiences nationwide.

OWN YOUR MEMBER TOUCHPOINTS

Every place a member interacts with the Gold's brand — the app, the website, the check-in experience, the booking flow — Gold's must control that experience. The brand defines the member journey; software adapts to serve it, not the other way around. In the Assemble model, vendor systems power the engine behind the scenes while Gold's-branded touchpoints deliver a unified, consistent experience across all 400+ locations. No vendor's default UI should dictate how a Gold's member experiences the brand.

KEY PRINCIPLES
  • Brand-controlled touchpoints — every member-facing experience reflects Gold's brand, not vendor defaults
  • API-first architecture — every system connects via standard APIs
  • Data ownership — Gold's owns the integration layer and data model
  • Best-in-class selection — each component chosen for category strength
  • Corporate as super-franchisee — same platform, no parallel systems
  • Vendor-portable architecture — any component can be swapped or upgraded without rebuilding the stack
  • Asset-light delivery — vendor-managed infrastructure, Gold's manages relationships

04 STRATEGIC QUESTIONS FOR PHASE 1

These questions must be resolved during the onsite strategy sessions to lock the Venn diagram and move into the blueprint phase.

  1. What is the Gold's Gym "box of the future" — what amenities are required vs. optional?
  2. What services run from Gold's Gym corporate vs. localized franchisee options?
  3. How will Gold's corporate clubs be run/operated vs. the franchisor services model?
  4. What is the budget for an assemble/hybrid option — will development gaps be in-house or outsourced?
  5. How will AI be used in this system and what guardrails need to be in place?
  6. How will the data-first architecture be set up and who owns it?
  7. What org chart and reporting structure needs to be in place for this model?
  8. Where do MarTech and Product sit organizationally — under Technology or Marketing?
  9. Is the tech/support staff separate for franchisee support, or shared with corporate?
  10. How is the initiative funded — tech fees, royalties, franchise model?
