02 ORGANIZATIONAL EXPANSION

The platform requires a dedicated operating team. This is not a massive engineering buildout — it's a focused team that manages the Assemble model.

  • BI and reporting infrastructure
  • Analytics and insights
  • AI/ML pipeline management
INTEGRATION TEAM

  • Vendor integration and onboarding

PRODUCT TEAM

  • Consumer experience design

  • User research and testing
DIGITAL OPERATIONS
  • Franchisee support and onboarding
  • Vendor relationship management
  • Training and documentation
  • Platform operations and SLA management
ASSET-LIGHT STRUCTURE

This is not a 50-person engineering department. It's a focused team that manages vendors, owns the integration layer, and operates the platform. Corporate-owned locations are treated like franchisees — they use the same platform, same processes, same tools. The Assemble model keeps the team lean because vendors handle their own infrastructure and development.

03 VENDOR SOP & PARTICIPATION

Every vendor that participates in the Gold's Gym platform must meet a consistent set of standards. No exceptions.

RequirementStandardPurpose
Onboarding Standard integration playbook with defined milestones Consistent, predictable vendor integration
API Integration RESTful APIs conforming to Gold's data model Seamless data flow into central platform
Data Ownership All data belongs to Gold's; vendor has usage rights only Protect member data and prevent vendor lock-in
Performance SLA with uptime, response time, and support requirements Reliable platform experience for franchisees
Compliance Alignment with Gold's security, privacy, and brand policies Risk management and regulatory compliance
Exit Strategy Data export and migration plan required in every contract Ability to swap vendors without losing data or continuity

All vendors operate within our system, not outside of it. This governance framework is not optional. It's the mechanism that makes the Assemble model work at scale. Vendors that can't meet these standards don't participate.

Every major vendor is now bundling AI agents into their stack. The question for Gold's: do we pay vendors for their agents, or do we own our own? Our recommendation is that the brand owns its agents — member experience, recommendations, and personalization are core IP, not features to rent. 07 UNIFIED DATA LAYER

The data layer is the foundation. Every member transaction, app interaction, web event, and operational signal flows through it — and the choice of platform here shapes what's possible at every layer above (CRM, BI, ML, member experience). This section frames the four platforms in scope and the criteria for selecting among them; the full analysis lives in a companion appendix.

PLATFORMS IN SCOPE
Platform Compute Model Best For Idle Cost Power BI Operational Complexity
Microsoft Fabric Pooled Capacity Units (CUs) Microsoft / Azure-integrated orgs; BI-heavy workloads Capacity billed continuously unless paused Native — DirectLake mode is Fabric-exclusive Medium
Databricks DBU + cloud VM (dual-layer) ML / AI + advanced data engineering + streaming Auto-terminate clusters; near-zero when off Connector + extra licensing High
Snowflake Credits (per-second warehouses) Multi-cloud, SQL-first analytics Auto-suspend; zero when idle Connector + extra licensing Low–Medium
AWS Redshift Node-hours (provisioned) or RPU-hours (Serverless) AWS-native orgs; steady SQL analytics Serverless: zero · Provisioned: continuous Connector + extra licensing Medium
DECISION CRITERIA
  • Existing cloud commitment — an active Azure MACC, AWS EDP, or Google CUD can swing effective platform cost by 20–40%
  • BI tool of record — Power BI workloads benefit disproportionately from Fabric (DirectLake mode; viewer licenses bundled at F64+)
  • ML / AI workload weight — heavy Spark / model training shifts the balance toward Databricks
  • Concurrency & real-time needs — streaming and high-concurrency interactive workloads price differently across platforms
  • Team skill profile — existing Azure / Power BI engineers vs. Snowflake admins vs. Databricks specialists materially impact ramp time and staffing cost
  • Compliance posture — HIPAA, PCI-DSS, SOC 2 may eliminate lower tiers and require specific editions
INDICATIVE SIZING (GOLD'S FOOTPRINT)

Gold's profile — ~400 US locations, member transactions, app and web analytics, operational reporting — sits in the Medium tier under the comparison framework: 1–50 TB stored, 100 GB–1 TB/day ingest, moderate concurrency.

Indicative all-in monthly TCO at this tier (platform + amortized staffing) ranges roughly $33K–$89K/month across the four platforms, with the spread driven primarily by staffing (specialized engineers) and reserved / commitment terms.

Final ranges narrow significantly once 3–4 of the open sizing questions are answered (data volume, workload mix, existing commitments, BI tool).

OPEN QUESTIONS BEFORE FINAL SELECTION

Platform selection should be driven by answers to a small set of sizing and constraint questions: data volumes today and 12-month projection, workload mix (batch / interactive SQL / ML / streaming), concurrency, existing cloud commitments, compliance requirements, BI tool, real-time freshness needs, team composition, multi-cloud requirements, and contract term flexibility. The full appendix walks through all ten.
