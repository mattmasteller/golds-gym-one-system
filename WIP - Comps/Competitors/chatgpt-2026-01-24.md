# Fitness Industry Technology Stack Competitive Analysis

## Scope and stack taxonomy

This research maps **publicly evidenced** technology vendors and platforms used by selected major fitness brands across **12 stack categories**: core club management, billing, mobile apps, CRM/lead management, access/check-in, group scheduling, PT management, marketing automation, website platform, digital/on-demand content, franchise management, and data/analytics.

Two recurring realities materially affect confidence and “Unknown” rates:

Most large chains operate through a mix of corporate-owned locations, franchisees, and regional operators, producing **non-uniform systems by market** (or by time period). This is explicit for some franchisors (e.g., F45’s own help content notes studios may use different booking providers). citeturn13search18  
Several stack components (payments gateways, access-control hardware models, web CMS) are often **not named publicly** unless there is a partnership announcement, a vendor case study, or a franchise disclosure document (FDD) requirement.

## Method and evidence rules

Sources were prioritized as requested: official company announcements, partner/vendor case studies, credible third-party industry reporting, and job postings that explicitly describe systems. For example, Planet Fitness’ Salesforce footprint is supported by a Planet Fitness “Salesforce Administrator” job description that references Salesforce administration and Service Cloud case-queue support. citeturn8view0

“Unknown” is used when no credible, brand-specific public evidence could be found for a category using the available sources during this run.

A note on PDFs: several franchise documents and ROI studies were available as PDFs and were parsed successfully via the web tool; the PDF screenshot function repeatedly returned a validation error in this environment, so citations reference the parsed PDF text view where used.

## Patterns and competitive implications

ABC Fitness is heavily represented in “HVLP/big-box + franchise” back-office modernization stories, often anchored in revenue-cycle outcomes. Crunch’s extension emphasizes ABC’s revenue-cycle management as a resilience tool (billing continuity, freezes/unfreezes) and explicitly links the partnership to billing/payments operations. citeturn9view0 Planet Fitness’ announced upgrade to **ABC Ignite** across U.S./Canada locations signals a major commitment to standardizing club technology operations at scale. citeturn34search17

Salesforce appears most clearly as an enterprise CRM backbone for higher-touch/luxury brands and scaled franchisors with central “club support center” operations. Equinox’s Salesforce customer story claims Salesforce powers sales pipeline, marketing, and customer service from a unified member-data view. citeturn23search1 Planet Fitness’ job posting similarly demonstrates internal Salesforce administration and Service Cloud operations support. citeturn8view0

Mindbody shows up as a “single platform” strategy for boutique franchise networks (F45) and as a widely used scheduling/booking surface for studio-style operations (Orangetheory studio pages appearing on Mindbody). citeturn12view0turn14search10 Importantly, F45’s own member help content indicates that studios may use **Mindbody or Glofox** as the booking provider, which is a practical signal of franchise-level variability even inside a “platform partnership” narrative. citeturn13search18

Modern “member experience” stacks increasingly separate (or augment) traditional gym/back-office systems with specialized engagement tooling. Equinox’s Braze case study is a vivid example: Braze is used to personalize in-app experiences and promote on-demand content to members whose prior app behaviors were oriented toward check-ins and class search. citeturn24view0

## Recent changes, migrations, and exclusivity signals

Planet Fitness: publicly stated that its relationship with ABC began in 2014, and the 2023 announcement extends the partnership through 2027 while upgrading to ABC Ignite across U.S./Canada clubs—suggesting a multi-year standardization effort rather than incremental tooling. citeturn34search17

Crunch Fitness: the June 21, 2021 announcement frames ABC as a comprehensive operational platform partner and highlights revenue-cycle/billing continuity as a core capability, implying a deep embedding of ABC into core operations. citeturn9view0

F45: a five-year Mindbody partnership was reported in January 2020, explicitly covering integrated software and payments across studio operations globally, plus online booking through studio websites or the Mindbody app. citeturn12view0 Yet F45’s help content suggests the booking provider can vary by studio (Mindbody/Glofox). citeturn13search18

Anytime Fitness: the combination of (a) an official billing FAQ naming **ABC Financial** as the billing provider and (b) the FDD’s inclusion of an **ABC Merchant Services Agreement** and **Club OS** agreement acceptance indicates mandated/approved vendor relationships and suggests comparatively tight franchisor control over core systems. citeturn1search14turn28view1turn28view4 Additionally, a press release explicitly calls Club OS the exclusive prospect and training management software vendor for nearly 3,000 Anytime Fitness franchised locations in the U.S. and Canada (at the time). citeturn26search9

Equinox: third-party advisory content (RSM) describes Equinox’s transition of critical systems to cloud solutions including Salesforce and Microsoft Dynamics 365, implying multi-platform enterprise modernization rather than a single-suite approach. citeturn24view2

Blink Fitness: 2024 bankruptcy and subsequent PureGym acquisition likely drove or accelerated stack changes; Reuters reports the asset acquisition (corporate operations and dozens of gyms), which in practice often triggers platform consolidation. citeturn38view2 In current PureGym U.S. app distribution, the Android listing shows the app is published by **Innovatise GmbH**, a detail useful for post-acquisition mobile platform inference. citeturn38view0

## Research gaps and what to pull next

Large gaps remain for several brands (especially LA Fitness, Gold’s Gym as a whole, many YMCA markets, and Blink’s pre-acquisition stack) in the categories that are typically least public: payment processor rails, access-control hardware vendors, website CMS/hosting, and franchise reporting platforms. This is a function of public disclosure norms rather than lack of technology.

To close the gaps with high confidence, the fastest path is: franchisor FDD tech exhibits (where accessible), recent corporate IT job postings (explicit “must have experience with X”), and vendor case studies for access control (e.g., palm/biometric, door controllers, turnstiles), marketing automation, and data stacks.

Where franchise documents are available but long, focus search inside them on: “Club Management Software,” “Billing Processor,” “Merchant Services,” “CRM,” “Access Control,” “Door Reader,” “Mobile App,” “Required Vendor,” and “Approved Supplier.” Anytime Fitness’ FDD demonstrates that these documents often enumerate vendor agreements as exhibits (e.g., ProVision, Club OS, Evolt, ABC Merchant Services). citeturn28view1turn28view4
