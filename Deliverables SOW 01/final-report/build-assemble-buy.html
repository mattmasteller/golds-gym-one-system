01 THE THREE MODELS

Market context: Our competitive analysis reveals that every major fitness franchisor — Crunch, Planet Fitness, Anytime, Xponential — assembles their stack from best-in-class components. No brand has successfully built or bought a monolithic solution. Below are Gold's three options evaluated against this reality.

How should Gold's Gym acquire its next-generation technology platform? Three fundamental approaches exist, each with real precedent in the fitness franchise industry.

BUILD
  • What: Custom-build a proprietary platform tailored to Gold's exact specifications
  • Timeline: Longest path to value — measured in years, not months
  • Cost: Highest upfront and ongoing investment
  • Team: Large — dedicated engineering, product, QA, and DevOps
  • Control: Complete ownership of code, roadmap, and IP
  • Risk: Highest — talent acquisition, scope creep, maintenance burden
  • Who does it: Very few — corporate enterprises and some boutique chains with VC backing
The timeline, cost, and team requirements don't align with Gold's current priorities and operating structure.
ASSEMBLE
  • What: Select best-in-class vendors per category, integrate via owned API layer
  • Timeline: Moderate — months to pilot, not years
  • Cost: Moderate upfront integration investment plus ongoing vendor SaaS fees
  • Team: Medium — integration architect, project manager, vendor managers
  • Control: Own data model, integration layer, and member-facing experiences; software bends to the brand, not the reverse
  • Risk: Moderate — integration complexity, vendor coordination
  • Who does it: Xponential (Mariana Tek + integrations), Crunch (ABC + custom layers), Lift Brands (multi-vendor stack)
Asset-light by design. Matches Gold's DNA. How every successful fitness franchisor operates.
BUY
  • What: Select a single vendor to provide all functionality as a unified suite
  • Timeline: Fastest deployment — but limited by vendor capabilities
  • Cost: Lowest upfront — per-location SaaS fees
  • Team: Small — vendor relationship management only
  • Control: Minimal — locked into vendor's roadmap and release cycle
  • Risk: High — vendor dependency, all eggs in one basket
  • Who does it: Smaller franchise brands with simple requirements
No single vendor covers all Gold's requirements. Current ABC/Motionsoft scores (2.0-2.3/5) prove single-vendor gaps.

Key principle across all models: Gold's must own its data and integration layer regardless of approach. Current system scores (2.0-2.5/5 for core platforms vs. 4.5/5 for best-in-class Motion Vibe) demonstrate why the Buy model fails and category-specific selection wins. See full system scores →

ASSET-LIGHT BY DESIGN

The Assemble model requires a medium-sized team — not the large headcount of a Build approach. Gold's manages vendor relationships and owns the integration strategy, while vendors manage their own infrastructure, support, and product development. This is the same model that Xponential Fitness and Lift Brands use to operate technology platforms across hundreds of locations.

03 FRANCHISOR SERVICES TO CONSIDER
REVENUE OPPORTUNITY & FUNDING GAP

Gold's currently collects $0 in technology fees or rebates from franchisees. Competitors like Crunch charge $5 per online join, percentage of billing as rebate, and tech fees built into franchise agreements. Establishing a tech fee structure is both a critical funding gap and a significant revenue opportunity — it funds the support infrastructure and aligns Gold's with every major competitor's franchise model.
