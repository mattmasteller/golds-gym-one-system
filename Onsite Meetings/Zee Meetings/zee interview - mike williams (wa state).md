## Business Model & Operations

### Pricing Structure
- **Seattle Area**: $58-$65/month (just raised Kirkland)
- **Vancouver Area**: Starts lower for annual vs month-to-month
- **Guest Option**: $85-$95/month (bring a guest)
- **Enrollment**: $100 (sometimes reduced to $50 or $0)

### Sales Model
- **No traditional sales team** - transitioned pre-COVID
- iPad/online join model
- No discounting on monthly dues
- Offers focus on free time or reduced enrollment
- "Ease of entry" approach: $1 to start, billing begins in 7 days

### Operational Philosophy
> "I'm very good at sales, but this industry, it's just terrible what we do to people."

- Hired VP of Sales specifically to transition AWAY from sales mentality
- Promoted operational people to management
- Rolled out new model during COVID closure

## Key Insights & Perspectives

### On the Corporate Website (Detailed)

**The Core Problem:**
> "Once they left Gold's Gym [site], we wouldn't know where they went. So there's no tracking of them. Individual. There's no capturing of that data. There's no retargeting. And so we lose all that."

**Technical Issues:**
- Location pages link out to ABC join forms
- Once user leaves Gold's domain, all tracking is lost
- No abandoned cart capture
- No pixel tracking on ABC pages
- Can't post schedules for non-standard programs (swim lessons, etc.)
- Site becomes "gateway to off-site" rather than conversion tool

**Recent Progress:**
- Just moved to new online join system (yesterday at time of interview)
- Initial box captures name before showing plans
- Already seeing abandoned cart data for first time
- Some staff reporting "leads already doubled"

**Comparison to Automotive:**
> "When you go to BMW USA, you get this site, but when you go to BMW Bellevue... they look like they are the parent of the site. Ours is reversed. Ours is everything's up here and you guys are all just parts of it."

**Impact:**
> "Franchisees with their own website are saying they're getting eight to nine more memberships a day in some cases."

### On the Franchise Structure

**Critical Assessment:**
> "They haven't figured out how to be a franchisor. And our systems are definitely not geared for franchising."

**The Problem:**
> "Our website is really built for the company-owned gyms. And that's what everybody's realized."

**Self-Serving vs. Brand Growth:**
> "Individually as an owner, we might feel like that's great because it's self-serving, but now we're at a point where the brand... has outgrown us because we haven't grown, and we haven't grown because we're not a marketable investment capital growth type model."

### On Other Franchisees

**Perspective:**
> "You guys don't have the vision. You are looking at your business and your model... And I understand that's your livelihood, but what you're not realizing is down the road, the decisions you're making for this brand and preventing it from growing and doing that is actually hurting you."

**SoCal Acquisition:**
> "The SoCal acquisition might be the best tipping point we might get. Unfortunately, losing them would have been good, but it might be what it takes to show everybody."

### On RSG/Corporate

**After Rainer's Passing:**
> "With the passing of Rainer, a lot happened at RSG... nothing's been as smooth and nothing has been as go-getting."

**Website Timeline:**
> "It's been two years of them talking about it and they still haven't got it done. And that's insane in today's market."

**Third-Party Issues:**
- Original website built by German third-party company
- "Hired the wrong tech company"
- Now working with another tech company

### On Being a "Test Dummy"

**Role:**
> "I've been the guinea pig and I've always been the guinea pig kind of for the brand."

**Why:**
- Makes "okay money" in Gyms, made good money at Cigna
- Not as strapped financially as some franchisees
- Tries to show corporate what works with franchisees

**Examples:**
- Followed whatever media company corporate uses
- Followed W Promote
- Let them use his data for API testing
- First to test new website features
